# Phase B: Synthetic Media Policy

## University Communications Office

### 1. Purpose

This policy establishes rules for the responsible creation and use of synthetic media by a university communications office. Synthetic media can make communication faster, more accessible, and more convenient, but it can also create risks involving misinformation, consent, impersonation, and loss of context.

The purpose of this policy is not to prohibit useful applications of artificial intelligence. It is to establish clear boundaries so that synthetic audio, video, and other AI-generated representations are used transparently and with human oversight.

### 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, student workers, contractors, and other individuals creating synthetic media on behalf of the university communications office.

For this policy, synthetic media includes AI-generated or substantially AI-modified audio, video, voice, or human likenesses intended for communication with students, employees, prospective students, alumni, the media, or the general public.

Routine text assistance, such as using AI to brainstorm or edit internal written material, is outside the scope of this policy unless the resulting content falsely represents a real person.

### 3. Permitted Uses

Synthetic media may be used when there is a legitimate university communication purpose and the content has been reviewed before publication.

Examples of permitted uses include:

- Generic synthetic narration for verified university information.
- Accessibility versions of written communications.
- Narration of research or educational information.
- Synthetic narration for approved promotional or informational material.
- Translated or alternative-language narration when the underlying information has been verified.
- Use of a real person's synthetic voice or likeness when that person has provided documented consent.

Before publication, factual claims contained in synthetic media must be checked against the original university source or other approved source.

### 4. Prohibited Uses

The university communications office must not:

- Clone or reproduce an identifiable person's voice or likeness without that person's consent.
- Fabricate statements and present them as statements made by a real person.
- Create synthetic media intended to deceive an audience about who actually spoke, appeared, or participated.
- Intentionally remove or conceal required synthetic-media disclosures.
- Use synthetic media to knowingly communicate false or materially misleading information.
- Publish synthetic media when the communications office cannot reasonably verify the information being presented.

Disclosure does not make an otherwise prohibited use acceptable.

### 5. Consent Requirements

A real person's identifiable voice, face, or other recognizable characteristics may not be synthetically reproduced without consent.

Consent should be documented before production begins. The documentation should identify:

- The person providing consent.
- The type of synthetic representation being created.
- The intended purpose.
- Where the content is expected to appear.
- The period for which permission applies.

A person who has provided consent may request that future use of the synthetic representation stop. Previously distributed material may not always be completely retrievable, and this limitation should be explained when consent is obtained.

If consent is unclear, incomplete, or cannot be verified, the synthetic representation must not be produced.

### 6. Disclosure Standards

AI-generated audio and video released by the communications office must disclose that synthetic media was used.

When practical, disclosure should appear in both written or visible form and within the audio or video itself. The disclosure should be understandable to an ordinary audience and should not be hidden in lengthy terms, metadata, or a location viewers are unlikely to notice.

Disclosure should remain attached to the content when the university reposts, edits, clips, or redistributes it.

Employees should assume that disclosures can eventually be removed by third parties. Disclosure is therefore required but must not be treated as the only protection against misuse.

### 7. Provenance Requirements

When supported by the technology being used, the communications office should preserve available provenance information, content credentials, or other metadata identifying the source and synthetic nature of the content.

The office should also retain the original approved version of significant synthetic-media content along with its final script, approval record, consent documentation when applicable, and publication date.

Provenance information should support verification, but employees must not assume that metadata will survive every download, edit, conversion, or repost.

### 8. Review and Approval Process

Synthetic media intended for public distribution must receive human review before publication.

The employee creating the content must first verify the script and confirm that required consent and disclosures are present. The employee's manager must then review the final artifact before it is released.

The manager should confirm that:

- The information is accurate and can be traced to an approved source.
- Any identifiable person represented synthetically has provided appropriate consent.
- Required disclosure is present.
- The proposed use is permitted under this policy.
- The content is unlikely to mislead the intended audience about its origin or speaker.

If the manager cannot confidently determine that these requirements have been satisfied, publication should be paused until the concern is resolved.

### 9. Incident Response

If synthetic media produced by the communications office is altered, misattributed, stripped of its disclosure, or redistributed in a misleading context, the university should respond promptly.

The communications office should:

1. Preserve the original artifact and evidence of the altered or misleading version.
2. Determine how the content was changed and where it is being distributed.
3. Publicly clarify the authentic version when there is a meaningful risk of audience confusion.
4. Contact the relevant platform and request correction, labeling, restriction, or removal when appropriate.
5. Notify relevant university leadership when the incident presents significant reputational, operational, legal, or safety concerns.
6. Review the incident afterward to determine whether the policy or production process should be changed.

### 10. Refusal to Produce Synthetic Media

The communications office should refuse to produce synthetic media when the proposed use violates this policy or when the risks cannot reasonably be controlled.

The office must refuse a project when:

- Required consent cannot be obtained or verified.
- The project requires impersonating a real person without permission.
- The purpose is to make fabricated statements appear authentic.
- Required disclosure would intentionally be removed or concealed.
- The content is designed to deceive its audience.
- Important factual claims cannot be verified.
- A manager determines that the potential for misuse or misunderstanding is too high to justify production.

The fact that synthetic media is technically possible, inexpensive, convenient, or accompanied by a disclosure does not by itself justify creating it.
