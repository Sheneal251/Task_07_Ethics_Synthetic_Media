# Task 07: The Ethics of Synthetic Representation

## Overview

This repository contains my work for Research Task 7: The Ethics of Synthetic Representation. The project builds directly on my experience creating synthetic audio in Task 6.

In Task 6, I used a generic AI-generated voice to narrate information that I had already verified. The artifact was disclosed as synthetic and did not attempt to imitate a real person. Creating it showed me that synthetic audio can be produced relatively easily, but it also raised questions about what happens when the same capability is used with false information, without consent, outside its original context, or at a much larger scale.

Task 7 moves from creating synthetic media to thinking about how its use should be governed.

## Organizational Context

For Phase B, I chose a **University Communications Office**.

A university communications team may produce research announcements, campus information, accessibility content, promotional material, social media content, and other public-facing communications. Synthetic narration could make some of this work faster or more accessible, but the same technology could create problems involving consent, misinformation, impersonation, and loss of context.

I chose this setting because it connects closely to my Task 6 experience of using synthetic narration for research-related information while also providing a realistic environment in which an organization would need clear rules about when synthetic media should and should not be used.

## Repository Contents

### [Phase A: Ethical Analysis](./Phase_A_Ethical_Analysis.md)

Phase A begins with my Task 6 experience and then considers four ethical axes:

- Truth
- Consent
- Context
- Scale

It also examines disclosure, provenance and content credentials, detection, legal and regulatory approaches, platform policies, and professional and organizational norms.

### [Phase B: Synthetic Media Policy](./Phase_B_Synthetic_Media_Policy.md)

Phase B contains a proposed synthetic-media policy for a university communications office. The policy establishes rules for:

- Permitted and prohibited uses
- Consent
- Disclosure
- Provenance
- Human review and approval
- Incident response
- Refusal to produce certain synthetic media

### [Policy Limitations and Residual Risk](./Policy_Limitations.md)

This section stress-tests the policy and considers where it could fail, including human noncompliance, manipulation after publication, limitations of detection technology, and technological change.

## Reflection

The part that surprised me most was not simply that AI could generate a voice. I could still recognize differences between the synthetic voice and a human voice. What stood out more was how easy and convenient the process had become.

That experience changed how I think about responsibility. I do not think AI itself should automatically be blamed for harmful outcomes. The more important question is how humans choose to use the capability. A useful tool can become harmful when someone uses it to fabricate information, copy another person's identity without permission, remove important context, or distribute misleading content at scale.

I also came away from this task less convinced that there is one technical solution to the problem. Disclosure can be removed, provenance can disappear, detection can make mistakes, policies can be ignored, and technology can change faster than rules. Responsible use therefore still depends heavily on human judgment.
