# Limitations and Residual Risk

The policy in this project creates boundaries for how a university communications office could use synthetic media, but it cannot guarantee that synthetic media will always be used responsibly. Writing a rule does not automatically prevent someone from ignoring it, misunderstanding it, or finding a way around it.

## Human Compliance

One of the biggest limitations is that the policy depends on people following it. An employee might forget to include a disclosure, misunderstand when consent is required, or publish something before receiving approval. A person acting in bad faith could intentionally ignore the rules.

Manager review reduces this risk but does not eliminate it. Managers can also make mistakes, and a review process is only effective when the people involved take it seriously.

## Loss of Control After Publication

Another limitation appears once synthetic media leaves the university's control. A university could publish an artifact with appropriate disclosure, consent, and provenance information, but another person could download it, edit it, remove the disclosure, or place it in a different context.

The incident-response process can help the university respond, but it cannot guarantee that every copy will be found or removed. This means responsible creation does not guarantee responsible use after publication.

## Limitations of Detection

Detection technology also cannot provide complete protection. An automated detector can produce incorrect or uncertain results, and synthetic-media generation technology continues to change.

For this reason, the policy does not treat detection as final proof that something is authentic or synthetic. Detection can support an investigation, but important decisions still require additional verification and human judgment.

## Technology Changes Faster Than Policy

Synthetic-media technology is developing quickly. A policy written today may not anticipate a new tool or method that becomes available later. New forms of generation may also create risks that do not fit neatly into the categories described in this policy.

The policy would therefore need regular review and revision rather than being treated as a permanent solution.

## Residual Risk

Even if every employee follows this policy correctly, some risk remains. Disclosures can disappear, provenance information can be lost, outside actors can manipulate legitimate university content, and audiences can misunderstand what they encounter.

This does not make the policy useless. It means the policy should be understood as one layer of protection rather than a guarantee. Responsible synthetic-media governance requires policy, technical safeguards, platform responsibility, and human judgment working together.

The most important limitation is that every safeguard ultimately interacts with human behavior. Technology can assist with verification and organizations can establish rules, but neither can completely prevent someone who deliberately chooses to misuse synthetic media.
