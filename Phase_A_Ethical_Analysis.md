# Phase A: Ethical Analysis of Synthetic Representation

## 1. Returning to What I Built
In Task 6, I created a synthetic audio artifact using an AI-generated voice. After listening to the final result, I did not feel that the voice could completely pass as human. To me, there was still a recognizable difference between a human voice and an AI-generated one. Even when the audio sounded clear and polished, something about the delivery still made it possible to recognize that it was synthetic.

What surprised me more was how easy it was to create. Synthetic audio does not necessarily require advanced technical knowledge anymore. The tools make the process convenient and accessible, and I do not think that makes AI itself the problem. My concern is more about how people use these tools. AI can make work easier, but relying on it too much can also make us stop questioning, thinking, and checking things for ourselves.

Creating the artifact also made me think differently about what happens after synthetic media has been produced. Even if I create an audio file responsibly and disclose that it is AI-generated, someone else could potentially take that audio, separate it from its original context, or use it in a way I never intended. The creator does not necessarily maintain control once the artifact begins circulating.

That is also where I would draw a personal boundary. I would not create a synthetic version of another person's voice without that person's permission. Creating a generic AI voice for a disclosed project is different to me from making something that could cause listeners to believe a real person said something they never actually said. The technology itself can be useful and convenient, but using it responsibly still depends heavily on human judgment.
